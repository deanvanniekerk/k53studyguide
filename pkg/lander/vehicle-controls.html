K53 vehicle controls.
A clear purpose.

Learn what each control does before trying to remember a numbered diagram. This introduction focuses on a manual light motor vehicle; motorcycles, heavy vehicles and automatic vehicles have differences.

By K53 Study Guide · Updated 9 October 2026

Start with the three pedals

In a conventional manual car, the pedals are arranged left to right: clutch, brake, accelerator. Study their purposes as well as their positions.

Clutch
Disconnects engine drive from the gearbox when pressed, allowing gear changes. Operate it with your left foot. An automatic car does not have a driver-operated clutch pedal.
Service brake
Slows or stops the moving vehicle. Operate the brake pedal with your right foot.
Accelerator
Controls engine power. Operate it with your right foot. Do not confuse it with the brake simply because both use the same foot.

Memory check: cover the list and name the pedal you would use to slow down.

Connect the other controls to a task

Steering wheel
Changes the vehicle’s direction.
Gear lever
Selects the gear. Learn the role of the lever rather than memorising one car’s gear pattern.
Parking brake
Keeps a stationary vehicle from moving. Distinguish this from the service brake used to slow down.
Indicators
Communicate an intended change of direction. A signal does not give you priority.
Mirrors
Help you observe behind and alongside the vehicle. They do not show every blind spot.
Speedometer
Shows road speed. An odometer records distance travelled.
Wipers
Clear the windscreen so you can see ahead.

Practise purpose before position

Try two passes through an illustration. First, name each control while its labels are visible. Next, cover the labels and describe its purpose aloud. If you can name a control but cannot explain what it does, make that your next revision point.

Switch the direction of the exercise too: start with a task such as “keep the parked car stationary” and name the relevant control. This helps you recognise the same concept when a question is worded differently.

Quick check: service brake or parking brake?

To slow a moving vehicle, use the service brake. To hold a stationary vehicle, use the parking brake. Similar names do not make them interchangeable.

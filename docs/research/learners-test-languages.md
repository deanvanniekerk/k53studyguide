# South African learner's licence test languages

Researched: 8 October 2026. Purpose: inform the translation and language-selection issue. Sources below are government, municipal, and parliamentary publications.

## Supported languages

The Computerised Learner's Licence Testing (CLLT) system supports the **11 spoken official languages**. George Municipality explicitly says applicants can take the test in any of these languages in its 16 April 2025 launch announcement. Tshwane's earlier launch announcement independently makes the same claim. These establish actual test-language support, rather than merely translated booking instructions. [George Municipality](https://www.george.gov.za/george-municipality-launches-computerised-learners-licence-testing/), [City of Tshwane](https://www.tshwane.gov.za/?p=3510)

The intended list is **English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa, isiNdebele, Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana, siSwati, Tshivenda, and Xitsonga**. The municipal announcements give the count rather than enumerate the languages; identifying these as the 11 spoken languages is an inference from the government's official-language list. [Statistics South Africa, February 2026](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19168)

South Africa now has **12 official languages**, including South African Sign Language (SASL). The CLLT announcements still refer to 11. A 2025 government demonstration describes built-in features allowing Deaf applicants to test independently, but does not establish that exam questions are delivered in SASL. Do not describe the text-translation feature as supporting all 12 official languages or infer a SASL test from hearing-accessibility support. [Statistics South Africa](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19168), [Western Cape Deaf-applicant demonstration, 15 October 2025](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/western-cape-mobility-showcases-digital-learner%E2%80%99s-licence-testing-deaf)

## Availability limits

- **Testing method and centre matter.** The George announcement covers its CLLT rollout and says oral testing remains separate and requires departmental approval for reading/writing assistance. It does not establish the language options for oral or legacy paper tests. [George Municipality](https://www.george.gov.za/george-municipality-launches-computerised-learners-licence-testing/)
- **Rollout is not universal.** A Western Cape parliamentary reply dated 5 June 2026 reports 66 centres using CLLT, with Lutzville, Lamberts Bay, Uniondale, and Murraysburg still awaiting implementation; the remaining four were planned for the 2026/27 financial year. This is a dated rollout snapshot, not confirmation of those centres' status on 8 October 2026. [Western Cape Provincial Parliament, question 12](https://www.wcpp.gov.za/?q=node/21003)
- **No nationwide centre-by-centre language matrix was found.** The primary evidence confirms the CLLT system's language support in Gauteng and the Western Cape. It does not prove that every South African centre offers every language for every testing method today. Confirm a candidate's intended language and method with their DLTC before booking.
- **Application-page translations are different evidence.** The national government's FAQ says information on applying is available in all official languages; it does not state that tests can be taken in all those languages. Do not use that FAQ as the test-language source. [National government FAQ](https://www.gov.za/faq/government-services/how-do-i-apply-learner%E2%80%99s-licence)

## Implications for the issue

1. Design locale support to accommodate all 11 spoken test languages. Publish only languages whose complete app/content translations have passed review; Afrikaans is a sensible first pilot because of the existing Jedidiah workflow, not because the exam is restricted to English and Afrikaans.
2. Keep the app's available study languages separate from claims about a user's testing centre. Suggested wording if centre-language guidance is included: "Confirm your test language with your driving licence testing centre."
3. Treat SASL as a separate accessibility/media scope requiring investigation; ordinary English-to-text translation cannot implement it.
